# Nightclub Nova

The main project is **Club Nova**, an isometric nightclub tycoon game in `game/` (Phaser 3 + Vite). The repo root also has two Python art generators (`generate.py` for posters, `bar.py` for an isometric bar sprite). See README.md for the layout.

See ROADMAP.md for the plan and LEVEL_PLAN.md for the 1-40 unlock pacing (start plain, a small mix of unlocks per level, the cool and animated designs later so upgrades feel earned). The reference for all art, UI and gameplay is Nightclub City; art/REFERENCE_NOTES.md lists what we take from the owner's screenshots. **The main reference for designs and asset creation is the owner's five club screenshots in `art/references/main/`** (notes in REFERENCE_NOTES.md): study them for every new asset, and make variations of what's there (other colours, materials, takes) to fill the levels. Match its style with original assets; never copy its images, logo or name (the screenshots are for reference only, never used in the game).

## Working on the game

- Run everything from `game/`: `npm install`, `npm run dev`, `npm test`.
- `npm test` builds `dist/index.html` and runs `tests/smoke.mjs` in headless Chromium. Run it before every commit, and add a check to it when adding a feature.
- The build is a single self-contained HTML file (vite-plugin-singlefile, all assets inlined). It must keep working when opened from disk via `file://`.
- `ClubScene` is split across `src/scene/*.js` as mixin classes, merged by `applyMixins()`. Put a new method in the file that matches its topic; `applyMixins` throws if two files define the same name.
- New buyable items go in `src/catalog.js`. Sprites go in `src/assets/sprites/` and are listed in `src/assets.js`.
- Flat surfaces (dance floors, and later wallpaper) are drawn in code, not Blender: designs live in `src/floors.js` / `src/walls.js` and are added to the catalog with `floorTier()` / `wallTier()`. Painted wall sections are saved under `wallpaper` in the save. Regular floors (`FLOOR_PAINTS`, `paintTier()`) are painted tile by tile like wallpaper (`src/scene/floorPaint.js`, saved under `floorPaint`), under furniture too: while one is held, `fadeFurnitureForFloor()` makes furniture and its staff see-through so the tiles under them can be seen and clicked, and the hovered tile is outlined on top; dance floors (`floorTier()`) are placed props, and the only tiles patrons dance on. A `flow` design needs `frames` to be a multiple of `period`. Dance floors stay still until level 5, then light up gently (Soft Glow 5, Blue Pulse 7, Pink Pulse 8, Two-Tone Blink 11; `pulseFloor()` / `litPanel()`), and the fancy animated ones come from 14 up (Galaxy Swirl at 37).
- Sprite calibration (`displayWidth`, `originX`/`originY`) was measured against the actual rendered PNGs. Re-measure it if a sprite is re-rendered.
- The bare room is beaten-up torn wallpaper (`tornPaper` in walls.js, three variations picked per section, drawn under any wallpaper by `drawBareWalls()`; Old Brick and Brick are level 2 and 4 wallpapers; there are 23 wallpapers, the October 2026 batch from the reference clubs being Cinder Block 3, Subway Tile 6, Wood Planks 9, Theatre Red 14, Speaker Wall 17, Black Arches 21, Bottle Shelf 25, Purple Glow 29, Ice Panels 33 and LED Lights 35) and one seamless worn-concrete floor (`bareFloorCanvas()` in floors.js, `drawBareFloor()`), beaten up on purpose (`beatUpFloor()`: saw-cut joints, patches, spill rings, cracks, chips; the owner found the clean one too nice for a starter club). Floor tiles are invisible polygons, only tinted while highlighted. A new club (no save) gets `placeStarterLayout()`: the booth on the left wall with a 3x3 Basic Floor (the level 1 dance floor; Checker is level 4) in front of it, one Starter Bar with its bartender on the right wall, and a Standing Table (`standingTable`, a tall cocktail table with no seats, in Seating at level 1) in the corner at the front end of the right wall. Bar units side by side, facing the same way, join into one long bar worked by one bartender (`barGroup()` / `isWorked()` in staff.js); check `isWorked(rec)`, not `rec.staff`, for whether a bar sells drinks. How many bartenders you may hire depends on level (`BARTENDERS` in config.js: 1, then +1 at levels 5, 10, 16, 23, 30; `bartenderAllowance()`); extras can join a long bar already worked (up to one per unit), spread along it by `staffBarGroup()`.
- The club's name is `src/scene/clubName.js` (`CLUB_SIGN` in config.js, saved as `clubName`): a club without one (new, or an old save) is asked for it first (`#namePrompt`, can't be skipped; tests' `waitForScene()` names it), and it goes up on a big neon sign drawn in code (`flatSign()`, slanted onto the right wall's plane by `slantedSign()`) standing on top of the right wall, sized to fit it (`CLUB_SIGN.fromBack`; redrawn by `buildWalls()` when the club grows), bulbs chasing. The owner wants it on the right wall: on the left wall it blocked the view of the line outside, and a sign at the front corner looked wrong; the club is renamed from the pencil button in the player panel (`#renameButton`, `promptClubName()`); the sign itself isn't clickable (the owner asked for that). It's also the page title.
- The drink menu is `src/scene/drinks.js` (`DRINKS` in config.js: price, unlock level, fun 1-5, mix time, glass and colour for its drawn icon, `drinkIconSvg()`): every unlocked drink is on the menu unless taken off (saved as `drinksOff`; the last one can't come off), set in `#drinkMenu`, opened from the Drink Menu card (`.menuSlot`) first in the Staff panel. A guest's order is picked when the bartender starts mixing (`patron.order`, `pickDrink()`: random, celebrities the priciest), mixing takes `BAR.serveMs` times its `mix`, and `serveDrink()` charges its price and cheers by `MOOD.drinkMood + fun * DRINK_FUN_MOOD`. The level-up menu lists new drinks.
- The daily gift is `src/scene/daily.js` (`DAILY` in config.js, saved as `daily`: { last, streak }): the first time the game is opened on a day (local date, `dayKey()`), `#dailyBox` shows a 7-day streak and Collect pays that day's gift (`collectDaily()`; cash grows with level, day 7 adds a random unlocked decoration to the inventory); a missed day starts the streak over, and after day 7 it goes back to day 1. A new club gets it after naming itself. Tests' `waitForScene()` stubs `showDaily`.
- The How to play guide is `src/scene/tutorial.js` (`STEPS`): a card at the top (`#tutorialBox`) walks a new club through the basics (Build, placing, a guest's card, high fives, Staff, a party, popularity), a gold glow pulsing round the button each step is about (`.tutorialGlow`); steps move on as the player does them (`tutorialFlags`, set by `noteTutorial()` where things happen) or with their button; Skip ends it. Saved as `tutorial`; a new club (`freshClub`) starts it once nothing else is on screen; clubs from before it count as done; the ? button runs it again. Tests' `waitForScene()` marks it done.
- The way in stays open: `footprintValid()` refuses anything solid on the door tile or right beside it (floors are fine), and placing something where a guest stands steps them to the nearest free tile (`stepGuestsOff()`).
- The street outside (road, sidewalk, buildings, velvet-rope line, bouncer, passers-by) is `src/scene/street.js`, settings in `STREET` in config.js. The door stays near the back of the left wall (`doorTile()` in world.js). The doorway (`doorZone()`: tiles within 2 steps of the door) is kept clear: it's part of `keepOffTiles()` (with the bar lines and the tiles at each end of a bar), which every spot picker skips; spot pickers also prefer the least crowded options (`crowdAt()` / `leastCrowded()` in activities.js) so guests spread out; a guest who comes in first walks to a clear spot just inside (`entrySpot()`, `patron.entering`) before choosing an activity, and leavers always walk all the way out. Like Nightclub City, the line stands on the sidewalk outside the left wall, from the door toward the front, on a red carpet behind a rope; it's drawn on `streetBackLayer`, under the walls, `STREET.lineOut` tiles out so the wall hides about the bottom half of each person (never draw street people over the walls). People in line face the door (`faceDoor()`, which re-plays the idle clip so the back-facing pose shows). Passers-by walk all four sidewalks; the left and back ones are on `streetBackLayer` too. In headless Chromium the game's timers crawl (slow frames, capped delta), so tests call street and timer methods directly instead of waiting.
- The buildings across the street are modelled in Blender (`art/blender/build_buildings.py`, the owner wanted real businesses and apartments with a dark, neon nightlife feel): ten designs (hotel, VELVET cocktail lounge, brick walk-up, noodle bar with a RAMEN blade sign, karaoke with a SING blade sign, a dark walk-up with a shuttered shop, liquor store, diner, tattoo parlour, laundromat), dark brick and stucco, mostly dark windows, each lit from the street by its own neon colour (`spill()`), no edge lines (they read as grey wireframes on dark walls). Built in game tiles (no MODEL_SCALE), `DEPTH` 4 deep, front facing -Y; rendered at facing 0 (behind the right wall) and 90 (behind the left wall) as `bldg_<name>_<f>.png` with `bldg_<name>.json` (width, depth, and per facing the picture size and where the building's centre on the ground lands, `ox`/`oy`). The game lines both back roads with them in `STREET.buildingRow` order (`placeStreetBuildings()` in street.js, on `buildingLayer` just over the ground; the old drawn blocks, `drawStreetBuildings()`, only show if the pictures are missing). No outside upgrades to buy (the owner said so).
- The street crowd (street.js): some passers-by walk in twos and threes (`STREET.groupChance`), and `STREET.hangouts` small groups stand chatting on the club's front and right sidewalks (`formHangout()` / `breakHangout()`, one breaks up and another gathers every `hangoutMs`). They use the ordinary guest looks (`streetCharacter()`); the five ChatGPT girls tried as street people were taken out (the owner wants to sort out characters later). The `STREET_PATRONS` loader in assets.js (`patron_st*` sheets) and `art/characters/street/` stay for later.
- A DJ booth's footprint includes the DJ's own two tiles behind the desk (`backTiles` in catalog.js, tagged `.back` by `getFootprint()`); art and draw order use `deskTiles()`. A booth saved against the wall is moved out a tile on load. There are twelve booth designs (`boothTier()` in catalog.js, art in `art/blender/build_booths.py`, `--preview DIR name...` for a quick look): Wood 1, Crate 3, Pro 7, Brick 10, Club 13, Theatre 16, Neon 21, Truss 24, LED Screen 27, Ice 29, Art Deco 33, Holo 38.
- The club always has exactly one DJ booth with a permanent DJ (`ensureClubBooth()` / `upgradeClubBooth()` in `staff.js`); only bartenders are hired. Music is `src/music.js`. The Bass Boost and Drink Rush buttons are `src/scene/boost.js` (`BOOST`, `RUSH`): each shows a big popup (`showBigPopup()` in hud.js), Bass Boost flashes the screen and pulses light across the dance floors (`pulseDanceFloor()`), Drink Rush lights up the worked bars (`highlightBars()`); each raises a weight in `activityWeights()` for a while, and `rallyGuests()` has `joinShare` of the guests who can (`canRally()`: not leaving, arguing, in a bar line or already doing it) react at once and set off a moment later (`patron.wantActivity`, taken first by `chooseActivity()`). When a boost starts, everyone on the dance floor goes wild for `BOOST.exciteMs` (`exciteDancer()`: much faster dancing, heart eyes or ! popping up); the rest of the boost dancers dance a little faster (`energize()`, applied in `setPatronAnimation()`) and longer. Quick reactions over guests' heads (happy face, excited face, drink, note) are `popReaction()` in `src/scene/reactions.js` (`REACTIONS` in config.js), drawn as textures in code.
- The club runs one endless night like Nightclub City (no night clock or summary): `src/scene/rating.js` keeps `doorsOpen()` / `clubOpen()` always true, and every `RATING.sampleMs` rates the crowd's average vibe in stars (+ bonus fans); `clubRating()` averages the last few (saved under `nightStars`). Income goes through `noteIncome()` (running totals).
- Parties are `src/scene/parties.js`, the list in `PARTIES` in config.js. Each has its own look while it runs (`src/scene/partyFx.js`, `startPartyFx()` / `stopPartyFx()` from `startParty()` / `endParty()`): guests wear a costume piece drawn on them (`dressGuest()`, also for guests arriving during it; party hats, snapbacks and chains, neon glasses, crowns, glow necklaces, masks over the eyes, rave glasses; face pieces hide from behind; positions `EYES` / `HEAD_TOP` / `NECK` measured on the guest sheets) and the room gets an effect (confetti and balloons, gold notes, neon lights flying round, gold sparkles, glow specks, feathers, sweeping lasers). Nothing pulses to the beat. Their effects go through `partyEffect(name, none)` (capacity, arrivals, tips, thirst, fans); one at a time. Throwing one starts a `PARTY_COUNTDOWN_MS` countdown (`partyPhase` 'countdown', then 'running' for `PARTY_LENGTH_MS`; `currentParty()` is only the running one), shown on the banner. A party's job is to fill the club and boost tips, dancing and drinking (no celebrities). When it starts, its `crowd` joins the line outside via `callPartyCrowd()`, and the bouncer lets them in one every `STREET.partyAdmitMs`. When it ends, party guests leave over `leaveOverMs`, the party pays fans (`XP.partyPerGuest`, capped at `XP.partyMax`), and a wrap-up popup (`showPartySummary()`, `#partySummary`) lists what happened while it ran: `partyStats` (guests, celebrities, drinks, drink revenue, bonuses, average happiness from `samplePartyMood()`, fights / ejections) exists only while a party runs.
- The dock along the bottom is the game's navigation (`src/scene/shop.js`), laid out as folder tabs (the owner's pick, "Option C" in the Figma file Club Nova UI, https://www.figma.com/design/r20PfNgOWXi3T8yUSaqYYw; the folder-tab section at the end of style.css overrides the older dock rules): the pads (`#navBar`, `.dockTab.navPad`, `data-nav`: Build, Staff, Club, Inventory and VIP, the celebrities, `#navCelebs`) are low tabs, icon beside label (on narrow screens only the open one shows its name), sticking up from the top edge of a tray (`#dockCenter`) that holds the category chips (`.storeTab`, icon plus a short `.keyLabel`, `label` in `CATEGORIES`) and one swipeable row of items (`#shopItems`, no arrows); the open tab glows pink, the green OK (`#storeOk`, shown while a panel is open) is a tab after VIP. Closed, only the tabs show. Each panel (`setDockTab()`: 'decor', 'staff', 'edit', 'expand', 'inventory', 'celebs') belongs to one pad (`NAV_OF`; `activeDockKey()` gives the lit pad). Build ('decor') and Club have a row of keys on the panel's top edge (`data-mode="store"`, keys tagged with their `data-nav`): Club's are Edit and Expand (`CLUB_KEYS`; the Club pad reopens the last one, `lastClubTab`). Goals aren't in the dock: they're a tab on the left side under the player panel (`#goalSide`, `#goalTab`, placed just under the player panel) that drops the three goals down (`toggleGoals()`), closed by its x (`#goalsClose`) or the tab again. Icons are clean white glyphs in `ICONS` in `src/uiIcons.js` (keys give the colour); the dock pads, the cash and Bass Boost are detailed full-colour drawings in `ART` (`artShop`, `catStaff`, `artClub`, `artStorage`, `artGoals`, `artCeleb`, `artSpeaker`, `cash`), as are a few others (give each its own gradient ids, since browsers skip gradients defined inside hidden elements). The dock starts closed (`data-mode="closed"`: just the bar); clicking a pad opens its panel, clicking it again closes it, and OK finishes the current action and closes it (`closeDock()`). Build's keys: NEW first (`isNewItem()`: unlocked at the last two levels; those items carry a NEW tag everywhere), then the store categories (floors and dance floors share Floors; they unlock mixed together per LEVEL_PLAN.md). Edit's tools are Move, Turn, Put away, Sell and Clear Club (`EDIT_TOOLS`; Clear Club, `clearClub()`, asks first in `#clearConfirm` and puts everything but the DJ booth and bars into storage). The inventory and the Edit tools (move, turn, put away, sell) are `src/scene/inventory.js`; they work on floor tiles too (dance floor tiles are placed props; a painted floor tile goes through `editFloorTile()`: move picks it up to lay again free from the inventory, sell refunds half and takes its XP back, and the bare floor shows underneath); the inventory is saved under `inventory`, and things placed from it are free. The DJ booth can't be put away or sold, but Move picks it up with its DJ (`movingBooth`); calling the move off puts it back, and a save mid-move keeps it where it was. Things in the inventory place whatever their unlock level. With something in hand, a click places it, never opens a guest's card. While something is held, `updateGhost()` always shows it under the pointer, see-through, with the tiles it would take filled and outlined (`footprintFill` on the floor, `footprintOutline` on top): green where it fits, red where it doesn't (the picture tinted red too). What you're holding is named in `#selectedChip` at the top of the screen, clear of the floor. Cards that refresh often (Staff, Expand) only rebuild when their content changes.
- The room is `gridW` tiles along gx (the right wall) by `gridH` along gy (the left wall), not always square; check bounds with `inGrid(gx, gy)`. Expanding adds one row at a time along the front-left (`gridH`+1) or front-right (`gridW`+1) edge (`expandClub(side)` in world.js, prices and per-level wall limits in `EXPANSION` in catalog.js). The Expand tab shows the new row in green (`showExpandPreview()`) when a side card is hovered or picked, and a green Build it! card confirms. Saved as `gridW`/`gridH`; older saves' square `gridSize` still loads.
- Capacity is `patronCapacity()` in `src/scene/popularity.js`: it grows with **popularity**, not expansion (the owner's rule): `CAPACITY.base` (8) plus `perRootPopularity` x the square root of popularity, but at most one guest per `tilesPerGuest` floor tiles and never more than `max` (80). Popularity (`POPULARITY` in config.js, saved as `popularity`, shown as Popular in the stats panel) moves with every visit (`noteVisitPopularity()` from `patronLeaves()`: happy +3, content +1, unhappy -1, stormed out -5) and brings guests faster (`popularityArrivalFactor()` in `spawnDelayFactor()`). Old saves start at a popularity that keeps the guests they already fitted. Staff don't count, celebrities do (`guestCount()`); when full, arrivals wait in the line outside. The HUD shows it as "n/max" over a Guests label. Dance floors and parties don't add capacity. Expanding is locked by level, one row per wall every couple of levels (`EXPANSION.limits`: 10 at level 1 up to 26 at 40), so saving up cash can't outgrow the level.
- Levelling up opens a menu of everything that level unlocks, with pictures (`showLevelUp()` / `unlocksAt()` in hud.js); tests stub it out in `waitForScene()` so it doesn't cover the buttons. `iconSvg()` gives each copy of a drawing its own gradient ids.
- HUD buttons are drawn icons (`src/uiIcons.js`) with no words on them (only the dock's tabs and the shop's category chips have a name beside the picture); their name and description go in `data-tip-name` / `data-tip-text`, shown by the shared hover tip (`src/tooltips.js`). Put live state (timers, "club closed") in the tip text, not on the button.
- The HUD (index.html, style.css) is "neon candy", kept quiet so the eye goes to the club first: a few dark purple glass panels (`--glass`, `--glass-shadow`), dark screens (`--screen`) for dialogs and the shop items, and glossy candy buttons with a white rim and a shine, all from one shared rule that takes the colour from `--c` (round buttons, dialog buttons, song controls); hovering one makes it bounce (`boing`). Prices are gold pills, NEW/VIP tags tilted stickers, titles gold with a pink glow, a bold display font (`--display-font`); money and numbers use the clean `--money-font`, written like "$3,000" (`formatMoney()` in util.js). The stat meters bounce on the music's beat: `beatUI(kind)` in hud.js, called with every kick and clap from speakers.js, sets `body.kick` / `body.clap` and each stat box's `--meter`. Top left is the player panel (`#hudProfile`): the record with the level badge, the XP bar, the cash under it (it rolls up to a new amount, `rollCash()`, `.rolling` while it moves), tiny How to play / New club / Save backup buttons (Save backup, `setupBackup()` in save.js, shows the club as a code, `saveCode()`: `NOVA1:` + base64 of the save, and loads one pasted in, `readSaveCode()` / `loadSaveData()`, replacing the club), and a smaller song row with like, skip and sound (`#songBox`; how long the song has left is in its hover tip). Top right (`#hudRight`): the stats (rating, luxury, guests) in one panel and the Bass Boost / Drink Rush / Party buttons in one row under it, the same width. The right edge has the drink meter (`src/scene/meter.js`, `METER` in config.js): a glass cylinder of purple liquid (`--fill`) filled by drinks, tips and bonuses through `noteIncome()` (`meterIncome()`); full, it doubles drink prices for `METER.doubleMs` (`drinkPriceFactor()` in `serveDrink()`) while it drains, then empties. There are no zoom buttons: the wheel and + / - zoom. Every dialog is a `.summaryCard` or `.levelCard` with `.summaryButton` pads. Hover tips stay white speech bubbles. Money over guests pops up with `floatMoney()` in staff.js, always green (the owner asked for no red money); "+1.5x Tip!" rides above it while a boost or party multiplies tips; other floating words use `floatText()`.
- Guests with a drink (`pickDrinkingSpot()`) usually go and sit down with it (`VISIT.drinkSeatChance`, any free seat; `sitDown()` keeps them seated until the drink is done), else stand somewhere quiet near the bar, never on a dance floor tile. Guest visits are `src/scene/activities.js`: a guest stays `VISIT.visitMs` (4-8 min) and moves through activities (drink, dance, sit, chat, wander; durations in `VISIT`), picked by their kind's weights (`GUEST_TYPES`: Regular, Party Animal, Social Butterfly, Big Spender, High Roller, VIP Guest, Troublemaker; `pickGuestType()` by `share`, the big-spending kinds multiplied by 1 + luxury / `GUEST_LUXURY.per`; `tip` multiplies their tips in `tipAmount()`, `pricey` kinds order from the top half of the menu in `pickDrink()`, `vipSeats` kinds take a VIP booth first in `claimSeat()`; celebrities get VIP tastes; the guest card shows the kind); happiness (`HAPPINESS`): seats have a comfort of 1-5 stars by price, VIP booths +1 (`seatComfort()`, shown in the shop tip), and seated guests cheer up and sit `comfortFactor` times as much; admiring gives `admireMood`; a guest still happy when the visit ends stays `stayLonger` of it more, once; wanting something with nothing free costs `nothingToDoMood`; `patron.activity` holds what they're doing, and they leave once the visit is over and they've finished it. `pickRoamTarget()` hands off to `nextActivityStep()`, and arriving anywhere goes through `arriveForActivity()`. Every drink goes through `serveDrink()`, which starts the drinking activity. Bar lines and bartenders are `src/scene/bars.js` (settings `BAR` in config.js): a guest going for a drink (`joinBarQueue()`) takes a free service spot in front of any unit of a long bar (`patron.atSpot`), or waits in rows behind (`barWaitTiles()`) and steps up when one frees (`refreshBarLine()`). Bartenders walk side to side along the bar (`rec.staff.atUnit`, `bartenderWalk()`), each claiming a different spot (`unit.claimedBy`), take `BAR.serveMs` to mix, then serve (`tickBars()`), only to a customer standing still on their spot (`atServiceSpot()`), never one still walking or waiting behind. Bottoms Up! (from the bartender's card) makes that bartender mix and walk `BOTTOMS_UP.speed` (2x) as fast for `durationMs`, then recover (`bartenderSpeed()`); when a line gets long the bartender suggests it in a speech bubble over their head (`staffSay()`), not a banner. Nobody gives up before `BAR.patienceMs`; after that some leave with an angry face (`checkBarPatience()`). A guest thirsty for `VISIT.thirstGraceMs` wraps up what they're doing and goes for a drink. Stools are ordinary seats (the owner tried stools as bar service seats and asked for them to be removed). The UI calls it XP everywhere (the code and save still call it `fans`): it comes from guests (`XP` in config.js: +1 walking in, +2 a drink served, +2 a dance finished, +2 sitting down, +3 admiring a decoration, +3 leaving happy (`LEAVING_FANS`), +5 a troublemaker walked out), parties, and purchases: the **first** buy of each item only (`awardPurchaseXp(cost, x, y, key, major)` in economy.js, `boughtTypes` saved as `bought`; old saves count what they already own) gives `perDollar` of the price between 5 and 30 XP, or 40 for a major upgrade (an expansion row, a DJ booth upgrade, bartender training). Buying again, moving and selling give and take nothing (`revokePurchaseXp()` is a no-op), so buying and selling can't farm levels. Levels need more XP each time (`LEVEL_FANS`, 200 for level 2). Levels follow `LEVEL_FANS` (`fansToNextLevel()` / `fansForLevel()`).
- DJ, bar and seating upgrades are `src/scene/upgrades.js` (`DJ`, `BAR_TRICKS`, `BAR_TRAINING`, `DRINK_STOCK`, `VIP_BOOTH` in config.js): a better DJ booth entertains more (`djQuality()`, multiplies dancers' fun in mood.js); Song Dedication on the guest card (`dedicateSong()`, the DJ says it in a speech bubble, then a cooldown); Bar Tricks on the bartender card (`barTricks()`: bottles fly, guests nearby cheer, that bar's tips x`tipMultiplier` for a while, `barTricksFactor()` in `serveDrink()`); bartender training from the Staff panel (`trainBartenders()`, saved as `barTraining`, folded into `bartenderSpeed()`); drink stock (`drinkStockLeft()` / `useDrinkStock()` in `serveDrink()`, saved as `drinkStock`; the bars hold `base` + `perGuest` per guest the club fits, about 12-20 minutes of drinks; with none left nobody can join a bar line and bartenders don't serve; a bartender warns when low; restock from the Staff panel, `restockBar()`, $`costPerDrink` a drink); and a reservation fee when a high roller, VIP guest or celebrity sits at a VIP booth (`vipBoothFee()`, from `sitDown()`). Drop the Bass is on hold (the owner isn't sure yet).
- Guests have no lasting thought bubbles over their heads (the owner asked for them to go); only the quick fading reactions.
- Clickable bonuses are `src/scene/bonuses.js` (`BONUS` in config.js; `this.bonuses` holds every badge showing): every 45-75 s a happy guest holds up a high five or fist bump (a gold badge, not a reaction); clicking it within 8 s pays $88. Guests also now and then admire a decoration (the 'admire' activity in activities.js, `ADMIRE` in config.js: `admireSpot()` picks a reachable free tile beside one they haven't admired within `cooldownMs`; after `lookMs` they say WOW!/OUU! and offer a tip coin, `offerBonus(p, 'tip', amount)`). `bonusAt()` / `clickBonus()` run first in the pointer handlers, so a badge click never opens a card or places, moves or sells anything.
- Phones and tablets (`TOUCH` in config.js): input checks `pointer.button` (a touch has no `event.button`), a tap may wobble `TOUCH.tapSlop` px before it's a drag, a finger takes the hover state where it lands, a tap that just misses a guest or piece takes the nearest within `TOUCH.reach` (`objectNear()` in selection.js), bonus badges take taps a bit wider (`bonusAt(x, y, touch)`), and two fingers pinch to zoom (`startPinch()` / `pinchMove()` in world.js; the page itself never zooms or scrolls). On small screens the HUD is drawn smaller with CSS `zoom` (the Phones section at the end of style.css). The smoke test checks all this on an emulated iPhone.
- Hovering and clicking go by what's drawn under the cursor, pixel for pixel, nearest the camera first (`objectAt()` in `src/scene/selection.js`, settings `HOVER`): guests, bartenders, the DJ, the guard and furniture. Hovering one gives it an outline glow (Phaser preFX glow on its sprites: every layer of a bar, all of a three-tile booth). With nothing in hand, clicking furniture selects the whole piece (a green glow and its whole footprint outlined on the floor), the Edit tools act on the piece clicked anywhere on it, and a person opens their card (`clickObject()`); right-click sells the piece under the cursor. Bonus badges come first.
- Guests and bartenders are clickable (`clickPerson()` in `src/scene/guests.js`, which also has names, the info card and Bottoms Up!). The card follows Nightclub City's layout: stat bubbles on top, portrait and speech bubble, round action buttons underneath (seat at a VIP booth, send to dance, drink on the house) that grey out with the reason as their hover tip. Only `VIP_BOOTHS` in catalog.js can be chosen for seating a guest. Drinks go through `serveDrink()` in staff.js. Luxury is `luxury()` in economy.js (settings `LUXURY` in config.js).
- Bouncers and troublemakers are in `src/scene/security.js` too: the club has `this.guards` (`this.guard` is the first, the free house bouncer inside the door, who also breaks up arguments); more are hired from the Staff panel (`hireBouncer()` / `fireBouncer()`, `BOUNCERS` in config.js: one more at levels 8, 18 and 30, a hire price and a wage; saved as `bouncers`), each at their own post (`guardPost(index)`). `TROUBLE.share` of guests are troublemakers (`patron.troublemaker`): now and then they bother guests within `annoyRange` (😈 / 😠, mood down, `disturbances`); a free bouncer within `detectRange` of one who has caused trouble walks over (`goGetTroublemaker()`) and walks them out the door (`escortOut()`: ejected, +`TROUBLE.xp` XP), then back to their post. The Staff panel (`renderStaffCard()` in staff.js) is in titled groups (`.staffGroup`): Bar (drink menu, restock, training), Bartenders (a card per long bar) and Bouncers (each one, and a card to hire another); people show as round headshots cut from their sheet's first front idle frame (`headshotUrl()`), with their first name on top and what they do underneath. Guests and staff are all guys for now, so names come from a guys' list (`FIRST` in guests.js).
- Guests getting along or not is `src/scene/security.js` (settings `SECURITY` in config.js): neighbouring dancers sometimes dance together (`maybeDanceTogether()`); a chat now and then becomes an argument (`maybeArgue()`, 💢 icons, `patron.arguing`), the security guard (`this.guard`, posted inside the door, not saved) walks over and usually settles it, otherwise it becomes a dust-cloud fight and one guest is ejected (`patron.ejected`, leaves for no fans). One argument at a time, with a cooldown.
- The Celebrity List is `src/scene/celebrities.js` (the list is `CELEBRITIES` in config.js: key, name, unlock level, fame 1-5, look; `CELEB` for fees, liking, return times, tips and fans). A celebrity's first visit is by paid invitation (`inviteCelebrity()`, fee `CELEB.inviteCost` by fame, from the VIP panel; pending invites are saved as `celebInvited`). How good a time they have changes how much they like the club (`celebVisitOver()`, from `patronLeaves()`: their mood, a VIP booth seat (`patron.vipSeated`, set in `sitDown()`), a drink on the house (`patron.onTheHouse`), the club rating; leaving angry costs a lot). Once they've been and like it at least `minLikingToReturn`, they come back on their own, sooner the more they like it (`celebReturnMs()`, `celebDropIn()`, asked by each street arrival); at `regularLiking` they're a VIP regular. What each thinks is saved under `celebs` (`celebState`: { visits, liking }). They queue in their own look with a star (`addStarIcon()`); walking in shows a big "Celebrity Arrival!" popup (`welcomeCelebrity()`) and the crowd reacts (`celebFanfare()`: star eyes or excitement, and some throw tips). `patron.celeb` is their entry; the guest card shows their liking. The VIP panel (`renderCelebCards()`, the dock's VIP pad) shows each one's portrait, name, stars, a liking bar, a VIP tag for regulars, and their fee or status (`celebStatus()`: locked / available / invited / coming / inside); the level-up menu lists new ones. Parties don't bring celebrities; there are no regular VIP guests any more (old saves' `vips` are ignored).
- Achievements are `src/scene/achievements.js` (`ACHIEVEMENTS` in config.js: id, icon, name, text, stat, target, cash, xp; saved as `achievements`): milestones on goal counters (`goalStats`, e.g. drinks, happyGuests, parties, celebs, walkouts, bonuses) or live values (level, popularity, luxury, wall). `checkAchievements()` runs every 2 s and pays one at a time with a big popup; an old save from before them gets the ones already reached quietly (`achievementsQuiet`). The Trophies tab (`#trophyTab`, under Goals in `#goalSide`) opens the trophy wall (`#trophyBox`, `renderTrophies()`). Tests' `waitForScene()` stubs `checkAchievements`.
- One-time tips (`HINTS` in tutorial.js, saved as `hintsSeen`): after the guide, the guide's card (`#tutorialBox.hint`) shows a tip the first time something comes up (a bar with no bartender, drinks running low, a troublemaker, a full club), glowing round its button, at most one every 20 s; `tickHints()`, stubbed in tests' `waitForScene()`.
- Sounds for the newer features are in `src/sfx.js`: `shaker()` (Bar Tricks), `whistle()` (a bouncer walks someone out), `clink()` (restock), `partyHorn()` (a party starts), `trophy()` (an achievement).
- Party costumes are drawn once into a few textures each (`costumeTexture()` in partyFx.js, `COSTUME_BOX`) and worn as images: as shapes redrawn every frame on every guest they doubled the frame time on a phone. Keep per-guest extras as images, not Graphics.
- Placing on phones is `src/scene/touchPlace.js` (the owner found tap-to-place too fiddly on an iPhone): on a touch screen (`touchUI`, from the last pointer used), holding a floor item (not wallpaper or floor paint) puts it on a free spot in the open part of the view (`placeSpot`, `freeSpotNear()`, `openViewBand()`); a finger starting on or by it drags it (`grabTouchPlace()` / `dragTouchPlace()`), a tap elsewhere hops it there, dragging elsewhere pans, and nothing is bought until ✓ on `#placeBar` (under the name tag; ✕ cancels, ⟳ turns; `confirmTouchPlace()`). While it's held, `updateHoverFromPointer()` leaves `hoverTile` on the spot (`syncPlaceSpot()` from `updateGhost()`). With a mouse a click still places at once.
- Updates for copies left open (a phone's Home Screen app has no reload button) are `src/scene/appUpdate.js`: every build gets an id (`__BUILD_ID__`, set in vite.config.js, which also writes `dist/version.json`); the game checks that file every 5 minutes and when it comes back on screen, and if the build differs shows `#updateButton` ("New version! Tap to update"), which saves and reloads (`applyUpdate()`). Nothing to check from `file://`.
- Placement keeps the door open: nothing solid on the door tile or beside it, and nothing that would box the door into a pocket (`doorStaysOpen()` in placement.js: the door must still reach at least half the room's walkable tiles). Guests only start an activity whose spot they can walk to (`beginActivity()` checks `findPath()`), and a guest who can't reach their entry spot gives up on it after 15 s (`patron.enterBy`), so nobody parks on the door tile and stops the line.
- Goals are `src/scene/goals.js` (the chain is `GOALS` in config.js: id, text, stat, target, cash, xp). Three show at a time in the Goals drop-down on the left side (`#goals` in `#goalSide`, `toggleGoals()` / `goalsOpen()`; a finished goal makes the Goals tab cheer and puts a ! on it until opened, `seenGoals()`); progress is a counter bumped where things happen (`bumpGoal(stat)`), a best seen while playing (`noteGoalBest()`, checked in `tickGoals()`), or live (level, capacity, rating, in `goalProgress()`). `completeGoal()` pays cash and XP; saved as `goalsDone` / `goalStats`. Tests' `waitForScene()` stubs the payout so cash checks stay exact.
- Songs are `src/scene/songs.js` (the tracks are `TRACKS` in music.js, settings `SONGS`). The club plays **real songs** (MP3s in `game/public/music/`, listed in `SONG_FILES` in music.js). They are NOT packed into the single-file page (that made it slow to open): Vite copies them to `dist/music/`, published next to the page (the /test/ page uses `../music/`, `musicDir()`), and each downloads when it starts playing. Opened from disk (`file://`) the browser can't fetch them, so the made-in-code `SYNTH_TRACKS` play instead; the smoke test checks that, then serves `dist/` over http to check the real songs play. Add one with `python3 game/tools/add_song.py SONG.mp3 "Title" "Artist"`: it shrinks it to 112 kbps and measures its tempo (`bpm`, fine-tuned to 0.01 over the whole song so the beat doesn't drift) and first beat (`offset`), and prints its `SONG_FILES` line. `Music.playSong()` plays it through the Bass Boost filter and counts beats from its tempo so the speakers and HUD bump in time; each song plays to its own end (`songLength()`). Only songs the game may use: Pixabay Music is fine; the owner's Epidemic Sound tracks are for videos, not the game (don't add them unless the owner shows their licence covers games). Songs don't add to the page's size, so there's no hard limit on how many; the club rating is `clubRating()` in rating.js (`RATING`, saved under `nightStars`).
- Items taken out of the game go in `REMOVED_ITEMS` in catalog.js (type: refund); a save that still has one, placed or stored, gets that cash back on load (Royal Thrones, October 2026).
- Pacing was balance-checked with `game/tools/balance_sim.py` (a minute-by-minute model of a club over 40 hours; pass name=value to try other numbers): level 2 in about 6 minutes, 5 in about 40, 10 in about 3 hours, 20 in about 11, 30 in about 27; bars restocked about every 12 minutes. Late-game cash piles up faster than there are things to buy (prices top out around $1-2k); that's a known open question for the owner.
- The save format lives in `src/scene/save.js` under the key `clubNovaSave_v2` (v2 came with the finer grid; v1 saves don't fit it). Don't break existing saves; bump the key only if the format changes incompatibly.

## Art pipeline

- New props are modelled in Blender with a script in `art/blender/`, using `iso_rig.py` for the camera, lighting and rendering. Run it with a Python that has `bpy==4.5.4` and pillow.
- Models are built at the original scale (1 Blender unit = one of the old 64 px tiles); `render_facings()` scales them by `iso_rig.MODEL_SCALE` (4/3) onto the game's finer 48 px tiles, so a model covers about a third more tiles than its size in units, and footprints in the catalog are in game tiles (bars 1x3, DJ booths 2x1 (models scaled by `BOOTH_SCALE` 0.75 in build_booths.py), couches/pool table 3x1, curved booths 3x3). `iso_rig.make_bar_piece()` squeezes a bar module horizontally to fit exactly 1x3 tiles, so bars placed in a row line up into one long bar. Model the prop centred on the origin with its front facing -Y. Game +gx is Blender +X, and game +gy is Blender -Y.
- `render_facings()` renders facings 0/90/180/270 (the model rotated about Z), crops all four to one shared box, and writes `<name>.json` (displayWidth, originX, originY). The catalog imports that JSON, so never hand-tune these numbers.
- Check new art in the game with the footprint tiles outlined before shipping it.
- After rendering new art, run `python3 art/compress_sprites.py` to shrink the PNGs (keeps the build small).
- Decorations are in `build_decor.py` (the October 2026 batch from the main reference screenshots: barrel, street lamp, globe lamps, crystal columns, glow plinth, speaker stacks (with `CONES` entries), glass screen, liquid tanks, gargoyle, robot, cat statue, glass waterfall, pagoda; colour variants share one builder, e.g. `globe_lamp(colour)`). `--preview DIR name...` renders facing 0 only, for a quick look before the full render. For scale: a bar counter is 1.0 tall and a patron about 1.5. Avoid `metal=1` materials (they render black with no environment) and faces that sit exactly on top of each other.
- The Spotlight's glow is drawn in the game, not in its render (the emissive lens renders dark): `createSpotBeam()` in lighting.js adds a beam, halo and lens for any catalog item with `spotBeam`, using the lens position per facing measured from its PNGs (`SPOT_LENS`); the lamp tilts up and back, away from its front. Re-measure `SPOT_LENS` if the spotlight is re-rendered. The Disco Ball's glow (halo cycling colours, turning rays, sparkles) is drawn the same way by `createDiscoGlow()` (`discoGlow` in the catalog, ball position in `DISCO`). Speakers (`speakerCones` in the catalog) get drawn cones on top of their rendered ones that bump to the beat (`src/scene/speakers.js`; cone positions worked out from the model in `CONES` with the px-per-unit measured on the speaker tower; beats come from `Music.onStep`, or the song's tempo without sound). `createGlowFx()` / `destroyGlowFx()` add and remove all of these when a prop is placed, turned or removed.
- Every prop render gets thin Freestyle edge lines in a darker shade of each part's own colour, like Nightclub City's art (`iso_rig.add_outlines()`, called by `render_facings()`; `OUTLINE_STYLE = 'nc'`, `EDGE_DARKEN`; the owner chose this over thick black outlines, which `'black'` still gives). Glowing parts (emission) are left without lines so neon stays bright. Booth backrests sit flush on their seats (no overhang), as in Nightclub City's booths.
- Seating is in `build_seating.py`, with the newer booths and sofas (each its own shape) in `seating_designs.py`. Parts named `Back*` (backrests) go behind seated patrons unless, at that facing, they're nearer the camera than the seat closest to them; `Canopy*` parts (roofs, arches overhead) always go behind; everything else is the front layer, which the game always draws on top (`frontAlwaysNear`). Seated guests are hidden by whatever part of the piece is in front of them, from any side: the seating renders include a depth map per facing (`iso_rig.render_depth()`, game gx+gy per pixel), and `build_seating.py occluders()` cuts, for each facing and seat, the part of the piece nearer the camera than someone sitting there (`REACH` in front of the seat toward where they face, for their knees) into `seat_<name>_occ<seat>_<facing>.png` (offsets in the JSON's `occluders`). In the game guests sit on top of the piece, nearer over farther, and `addSeatOccluder()` (seating.js) lays their seat's cut-out exactly over the piece just above them; it's removed when they get up. Each piece lists its `seats` and `sitLift` in its JSON; a seat may have a third number, the way it faces in degrees from the piece's front (so benches can face each other). Booths are 3x3, sofas 3x1 and the Wood Lounge 3x2 (`LOUNGE_3X2`); every booth is in `VIP_BOOTHS`. Patrons sitting is in `src/scene/seating.js`.
- The ten themed bars (Tiki, Surf Shack, Retro Diner, Garden, Brewery, Speakeasy, Disco, Candy, Marble Lounge, Cyber; levels 4-19) are `art/blender/bar_designs.py`: every one shares the same skeleton (`shell()`: counter, aisle floor, back cabinet) and dresses it; each back piece is designed on the whole cabinet top, then `push_back()` squeezes it against the wall so the cabinet's front stays a little bar top (the owner asked for that). `--preview DIR` renders facing 0 for approval.
- Bars render in two layers too (`render_facings(..., layers=split_counter(root))`): `<name>_back_*` and `<name>_front_*`. The game draws whichever is nearer the camera on top, with the bartender between them. Any prop staff stand inside should do the same.
- Patrons are drawn art (front view left, back view right, on white): every outfit is the same base guy with different clothes and hair. `art/sprites_from_art.py IMAGE INDEX` cuts him into head, body, arms and legs and animates them into a sheet (idle/walk/dance/sit x front/back; layout, frame counts and fps in `patrons.json`). Sit frames keep the torso where it is standing and fold the legs up under the hips, so seats calibrated for a standing patron still fit; `art/recolor_patron.py` makes extra patrons by changing skin tone and clothes colour. Sheets 0-5 are the drawn outfits, 6-11 recolours. Front faces screen down-left and back faces up-right; the game mirrors both for the other diagonals. (`build_patrons.py` is the old Blender chibi generator, no longer used.)
- Girl patrons will use the owner's girl base model (chibi, T-pose, long hair, T-shirt and shorts), described in `art/characters/README.md`. Her 3D model is `art/characters/girl_base.blend`; `art/blender/paint_girl.py SRC LOOK OUT.png` paints it in one of its `LOOKS` (no UVs, so prints and fabrics are projected from the front) and renders the front. She isn't rigged or animated yet; when she is, follow the guy's sheet layout.
- Guests that move like the owner's own 3D model (`art/characters/character_model_1*`, rigged and animated in Mixamo; clips in `art/mixamo/model1/`: walk, dance, sit, sittalk, drink) but look like the drawn guests: `art/blender/build_patron_hybrid.py`. `render` (Blender, ~1h) renders every frame of the model's body, its own head removed, as a cel-shaded white shade pass with outlines plus a flat id pass (skin / shirt / trousers / shoes / soles / glass), and notes the neck point, head tilt and facing per frame (`art/build/hybrid/`, not committed); `compose N...` (PIL, seconds) gives each a drawn guest's head (cut from their sheet at the chin, 60% of the standing height above the soles; front or back view, mirrored to match the face) and that guest's outfit colours (sampled from their sheet), writing `patron_3d_hybridNN.png/.json`. The body is chunkier than the model (`BUILD` bone scales) and has clothing details marked in the id pass by rest-pose position (`add_details()`: cuffs / collar / hem as trim, waistband, trouser turn-ups, a shoe stripe in the drawn shoes' second colour); the id pass renders with one sample and no denoising so its key colours stay exact. The owner found the fully 3D look (`build_patron_model1.py`, `patron_3d_teal`, and the cel-shaded `concept_model1.py`) odd next to the drawn guests, so only the hybrids are in the game. Each sheet has its own grid in its JSON (frame size, anchor, `starts` per clip_facing, frames and fps per clip); `patronMetaOf(i)` in assets.js gives any character's grid, the drawn ones from patrons.json. They come after the 12 drawn sheets (`MODEL_PATRONS`) and make up `MODEL_PATRON_SHARE` of new guests. They have two more clips: `drink` (a pink glass in the left hand, played while drinking) and `sittalk` (half of seated guests, `SIT_TALK_CHANCE` in seating.js); drawn guests fall back to idle and sit (`CLIP_FALLBACK` in patrons.js). The sit clips are lifted so the hips are at standing height, like the drawn guests' sit frames, so seat calibration fits both. There's no standing idle clip yet: idle holds the drink's first frame until `idle.fbx` is added.
- **Every ordinary guest is now the ChatGPT-painted guy** (`GUEST_LOOKS = ['guy01']` in config.js; `PAINTED_PATRONS` in assets.js; empty `GUEST_LOOKS` brings back the old mix). Made by `art/blender/build_patron_painted.py`: `--clay DIR` renders the bare owner's model front and back for ChatGPT to colour in (keeping the exact outline), the drawings go in `art/characters/painted/<name>_front.png` / `_back.png`, and `build_patron_painted.py <name>` wraps them onto the model (front/back blended round the sides; hair on a head-hugging shell with crown tufts, cut out to the drawn hair, solid on top) and renders `patron_3d_<name>` with `build_patron_model1.py`'s clips and grid (`--preview DIR` for stills; `PREVIEW_ALL=1` every frame). I don't invent the look: the owner judged my own painting of the model bad, so new guests start as ChatGPT drawings over the clay renders. Compress the sheet after rendering. Celebrities and staff keep their own looks.
- The Neon Cartoon guy (the code-drawn style the owner picked) is a guest: `art/characters/neon/neon_rig.py` draws him as a cut-out puppet in SVG (head, torso, upper/lower arms, thighs, shins, shoes, each with a joint, in the same coordinates as `neon_avatar.svg`), front and back views, and poses him frame by frame for idle / walk / dance / sit (sit keeps the hips at standing height); `render_neon.mjs` renders the frames into `patron_neon01.png` with its grid in `patron_neon01.json`. Neon sheets (`patron_neon*`) come after the 3D ones in `PATRON_SHEETS` (`NEON_PATRONS` in assets.js) and make up `NEON_PATRON_SHARE` of new guests (`NEON_PATRON_SHARE_TEST` on the test link). Re-run both scripts and `compress_sprites.py`-style quantizing after changing him.
